# Real user feedback

**Source:** Director of the Revolving Water Fund at the conservation finance
non-profit where the author previously worked, and the author's former
manager. She runs the programme this application was designed around.

**Method:** the application was described and shown to her; this is her
written reply.

---

## The quote, as sent

> "This is so useful, particularly the reading of the conservation plans. If
> we can tweak this tool as we find barriers, we would certainly use it and
> encourage all of our outreach specialists to use it.
>
> I worry about farmers' trust of the numbers, and their willingness to enter
> data like a conservation plan into AI. Also, this wouldn't work for our
> Amish farmers — roughly 10% of farmers — since they don't use technology.
>
> Overall this would save a ton of early scoping conversations, and
> potentially drop the number from five project-defining conversations down
> to two. Let's talk more about your AI work and applicability to CIF."

*Lightly copy-edited for spelling and punctuation only: "specaists" to
"specialists", "farmers trust" to "farmers' trust", "amish" to "Amish", and
hyphenation of "project-defining". No words were added, removed or reordered,
and no meaning was changed.*

---

## What she validated

**The document reading, specifically.** Of everything in the application —
the county map, the practice ranking, the cost calculator, the agent team —
the feature she named first was the one that attacks the four hours of
document archaeology. That is the problem the tool was built for, confirmed
by the person who owns the process.

**Adoption intent, conditional.** "We would certainly use it and encourage
all of our outreach specialists to use it" — conditional on iterating as
barriers appear, which is the right posture and not a blank endorsement.

## The number she gave

> five project-defining conversations down to two

This is a user-supplied impact figure, and it measures something different
from the time saving. The four hours are analyst time. **Conversations are
the farmer's time, and the field staff's.**

| | Per project | Across 250 scoped |
|---|---:|---:|
| Project-defining conversations now | 5 | 1,250 |
| Projected with the tool | 2 | 500 |
| **Avoided** | **3 (60%)** | **750 conversations a year** |

Those conversations are what the twelve outreach staff spend 35% of their
time on. The reduction does not reclaim payroll — it raises how many farmers
the same team can reach.

## The limitations she raised

These are more valuable than the praise, and all three go in the report.

**1. Farmer trust of the numbers.** A figure produced in thirty seconds by
software carries less weight with a producer than one an analyst worked
through. The application's answer is partial: every result shows the
arithmetic line by line, names its source, and labels itself a screening
rather than a certification. That is a design response, not a solution.
Trust is earned by the person delivering the number.

**2. Willingness to put a conservation plan into an AI tool.** A real
objection about a real document. The application does not store uploads — the
text is read and discarded, and the response says so — but a producer has no
reason to take that on faith. This is a procurement and consent problem, and
it is not solved by an architectural choice they cannot see.

**3. Amish farmers, roughly 10% of the farmer base, do not use technology.**
A structural exclusion no interface change fixes. For that tenth of the
population the tool is useful only to the field staff member, who still
screens on their behalf and brings a paper answer back. Worth stating
plainly: **a 60% reduction in conversations applies to about 90% of
producers.**

## What this is evidence of

- A practitioner who runs the programme says she would use it, and name the
  feature she would use it for.
- A quantified operational change, supplied by the user rather than derived
  by the author: **5 conversations to 2**.
- Three substantive barriers named by someone with nothing to gain from
  flattering the project — one of which (Amish producers) the author would
  not have identified.
